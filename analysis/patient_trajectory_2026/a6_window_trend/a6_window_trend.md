# A6 - nested windows: trend, ascent, and how far the score actually moved

Window convention `endpoint < t_to_event <= start`, the same as A2, so the 30-5 window is A2's truncated window (asserted against it). Kendall tau uses every observed minute in the window. The ascent fraction, the change in score and the step decomposition use the 5-minute bin grid, each bin the mean of its observed minutes. Intervals are 2000-replicate cluster bootstraps, drawn once per family-by-cohort cell and reused across windows so the windows are comparable to each other.

**Two samples, and they are not the same one.** A window is a *cap* on the capped tau, not the span it is measured over: an episode observed only from minute 20 gives the same capped tau at 60-5 as at 30-5, which is why that column barely moves across the sweep. Every other column, and the tau reported first, is on the **spanning** sample -- the episodes whose observation actually reaches the window start. That sample is much smaller at wide starts and is selected on operation length, because the median observable time from monitoring start to onset is only 43, 44 and 52 minutes and just 40%, 43% and 47% of event episodes reach 60 minutes. Read the 60-start rows as a statement about long operations.

**Reading the three together.** The ascent fraction is closer to a volatility measure than a level measure. A window with a large change in score but a low ascent fraction and a large largest-step share did not climb, it jumped once or twice; the same change with a high ascent fraction is a climb. Directedness is the net change over the total distance travelled: +1 is a monotone rise, 0 a pure wobble, -1 a monotone fall.

**Do not read the narrow windows' decomposition.** Directedness and the largest-step share are defined on the steps inside a window, and the 10-5 window has one step, the 15-5 window two. Their directedness is therefore forced to +/-1 and their largest-step share to 1.000 by arithmetic, not by anything the model did. The median number of steps is in the CSV as `median_n_steps`; treat the decomposition as informative only where it is comfortably above two.
